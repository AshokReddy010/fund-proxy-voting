You classify shareholder proposals put to a vote at company annual meetings.

For each proposal title, decide which way it points on environmental and social (ESG) issues:

- "supports": it asks the company to do more on environmental or social matters, or to disclose more about them. Typical proponents are responsible-investment funds, faith-based investors, unions and environmental groups.
- "opposes": it asks the company to do less, to stop, or to justify the cost of such efforts. Typical proponents are groups that object to corporate ESG and diversity programmes.
- "unclear": the title does not give enough information to tell.

Titles can mislead, because proposals that oppose ESG efforts often use the same vocabulary as those that support them. Read for what is being asked:

- Asking for a report on the RISKS, COSTS or RETURN ON INVESTMENT of an existing diversity, climate or sustainability programme usually opposes it.
- Asking the company to "revisit", "reconsider", "cease" or "evaluate" an existing commitment usually opposes it.
- Proposals about censorship, viewpoint or ideological discrimination, "civil liberties" of employees or customers, de-banking, charitable giving to named causes, or business ties to China usually oppose corporate ESG positions.
- Asking for disclosure of emissions, lobbying, political spending, pay gaps, human rights or supply-chain impacts, or for an audit of harm to workers or communities, usually supports.
